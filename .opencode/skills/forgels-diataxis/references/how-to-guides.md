# How-to guides

Source: https://diataxis.fr/how-to-guides/ (fetched 2026-10-18)

How-to guides are directions guiding the reader through a problem or toward a result. They are goal-oriented and serve the user's WORK. They assume the user knows what they want to achieve and is basically competent.

A how-to guide must be written from the user's perspective, not the machinery's. It answers a human project: what the human does with the tools at hand to get the result they need. Guides defined by tool operations ("select options and press Deploy") are useless: they state what any competent user already knows and are disconnected from purpose.

A rich list of how-to guides frames what the product can actually do.

## Key principles

- Maintain focus on the goal. Action and only action: no digression, explanation, or teaching. Link to explanation and reference instead.
- Address real-world complexity: the guide must be adaptable beyond the exact narrow case, so users can adapt it to their needs.
- Omit the unnecessary: practical usability beats completeness. Start and end in a reasonable place; the reader joins it to their own work. (Completeness belongs to tutorials.)
- Provide an executable solution: steps in the form of actions, including thinking and judgement, not just physical acts.
- Describe a logical sequence with sense and meaning in its order, grounded in the user's activities and thinking.
- Seek flow: smooth progress, good pace and rhythm. Do not make the user hold thoughts open or jump between contexts needlessly. Anticipate them.
- Name titles exactly: "How to integrate application performance monitoring" is good; "Integrating ..." or a bare noun phrase is bad because the goal is unclear.

## Language

- "This guide shows you how to ...": state the problem or task clearly.
- "If you want x, do y. To achieve w, do z.": conditional imperatives.
- "Refer to the x reference guide for a full list of options.": never pollute the guide with exhaustive options.
