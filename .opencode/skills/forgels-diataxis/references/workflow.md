# Workflow

Source: https://diataxis.fr/how-to-use-diataxis/ (fetched 2026-10-18)

Diataxis guides documentation work as well as content. It discourages planning and top-down workflows in favour of small, responsive iterations from which overall patterns emerge.

## Guide, not plan

Diataxis is a map to check position and direction, not a plan to complete. It helps assess documentation, find problems, and judge improvements.

## Do not impose structure

Do not divide docs into four sections up front, and never create empty tutorial/howto/reference/explanation structures. Instead make small improvements where opportunities appear; at some point the changes will demand moving material under a Diataxis heading, and the top-level structure forms by itself. Diataxis changes documentation structure from the inside.

## One step at a time

Whatever the state of the docs, improve iteratively. Publish every step in the right direction immediately; do not hoard large tranches of work.

## Just do something (the cycle)

1. Choose something: any piece, even at random. Preferably small: a page, a paragraph, a sentence.
2. Assess it: what user need is represented? How well is it served? What can be added, moved, removed, or changed? Do language and logic meet this mode's requirements?
3. Decide: what single next action produces an immediate improvement?
4. Do it: complete that action, commit it, consider it done. Then restart the cycle.

## Organic growth

Good structure develops from within, like a living organism built cell by cell, not imposed from above. Documentation is never finished (it keeps adapting) but can always be complete: useful, appropriate to its stage, structurally healthy, ready for the next stage.
